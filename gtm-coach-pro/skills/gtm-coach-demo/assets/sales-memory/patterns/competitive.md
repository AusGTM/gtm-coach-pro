# Competitive — rollup

Competitors named across the corpus, where they show up, why buyers switch, and the counters
that won (with the actual move + evidence from the won deals).

## Clearwater (incumbent BI / dashboards) — named in 2 deals (Culture Amp won, mentioned SafetyCulture)
- **Positioning they hold:** established reporting/BI dashboards; "we already pay for it."
- **Why buyers move:** lagging, deal-agnostic. *"Clearwater tells me what already happened."* (Sofia, Culture Amp). It won't tell a rep a live deal is dying.
- **Counter that won (Culture Amp, 2026-02-10 → close):** reframe from rival to complement, then displace. jordan-rep never fought Clearwater on features — he moved the spend question to a risk question (one slipped 7-figure deal vs a dashboard licence), and let Sofia land it herself: *"One of those slipping is worth more than every dashboard licence we've ever bought."* By the SafetyCulture close, *"neither Clearwater nor the spreadsheet status quo resurfaced"* — the ROI case had taken the oxygen out of the incumbent.
- **Landmine:** a BI feature bake-off. It pulls you into the incumbent's frame — move to deal-level risk.
- **Trap-setting question:** *"When you open the dashboard, can you tell a deal is at risk — or only see what already happened?"*

## PipelineIQ (point competitor) — named in 1 deal (Deputy, winning)
- **Why buyers prefer us:** *"weaker on deal-level risk scoring"* (Nadia, Deputy).
- **Counter:** demo the risk-scoring depth side by side; emphasise early-warning signal quality.
- **Sample size: 1 deal — weak signal, watch for more.**

## In-house build / analyst team (status quo) — named in 2 deals (Airwallex lost, Employment Hero open)
- **Why it wins when it wins (Airwallex, lost):** the CFO viewed forecasting as an analyst job, not software, and there was no compelling event to dislodge the status quo. *"This overlaps what my analysts already deliver."* (David). The root cause was a miss, not a bad pitch: **impact was never quantified to the economic buyer** — it landed with Rebecca (VP Sales), never with David (CFO), so he never felt it.
- **Counter that won elsewhere (SafetyCulture, Culture Amp):** quantify impact in the *economic buyer's* own number *early* and pin a dated compelling event. SafetyCulture tied ~$2.4M of slipped pipeline to Dana's board mandate; Culture Amp put the two 7-figure slips (Coles, Qantas) in front of Priya before planning. Both times the status quo stopped resurfacing once the EB owned the number.
- **Open risk (Employment Hero):** Ananya (CFO) currently favours the in-house/analyst path and has never been on a call — the exact shape that lost Airwallex. Get her the ROI case directly, or repeat the loss.
- **Trap-setting question:** *"If your analysts already do this, what did the deals that still slipped last year cost you?"*

_Based on 23 calls / 6 deals. The status-quo/in-house "competitor" is the most dangerous — it killed Airwallex (impact never reached the CFO) and is live on Employment Hero now. "Counter that won" lines cite the specific won-deal call so the handling is evidence-grounded, not generic advice._
