# Airwallex Demo — 2026-02-04

**Attendees:** internal alex-rep | external Rebecca Hall (VP Sales), David Nguyen (CFO)

## Summary
The demo, and the pivotal call. Rebecca got David (CFO / economic buyer) into the room — the access
we wanted — but he arrived with his frame already set: forecasting is what his analyst team does, and
Cadence looks like paying for it twice. The product landed well with Rebecca and poorly with David,
and the talk ratio (0.58) tells the story — we presented at his objection instead of into it. He never
felt the pain the way Rebecca does, and the next step came out soft.

## SPICED captured this call
- **Situation:** Confirmed live — David restated that Deepa's analyst team already produces the
  forecast monthly out of Salesforce and that Clearwater covers exec and board reporting. "We're not
  flying blind here. Deepa's team hands me a number the first week of every month, the board's already
  got their Clearwater dashboards. And look — we bought Anaplan, what, two years ago? Nobody logged in
  after the onboarding. So forgive me if I'm not rushing to sign up for another one." (David)
- **Pain:** Never landed with David the way it did with Rebecca. Rebecca reinforced the ~15–20%
  forecast miss and the manual roll-up burden; David treated those as an execution issue for his team,
  not a tooling gap. The pain stayed at the champion's level.
- **Impact:** Still not quantified to David's satisfaction. When ROI came up he reframed it straight
  back to cost: "So I'm paying a piece of software to do the thing I already pay six people to do.
  Someone walk me through why that's not just a second invoice." (David) We showed capability
  (real-time pipeline health, forecast-vs-actual variance, rep-level hygiene) but never converted it
  into a dollar figure he accepted against the ~$80k.
- **Critical Event:** None — and David said so plainly: "This isn't a this-quarter decision for me."
- **Decision:** David (CFO/EB) is skeptical — "why pay for software my analysts already do?" — and
  is effectively the blocker. Rebecca can champion but cannot overrule the budget holder.

## Signals
- **Objection (not overcome):** "This overlaps what my analysts already deliver." (David) — the core
  category objection; we demoed features against it rather than reframing what software does that a
  human analyst team structurally cannot (real-time, every-rep, every-deal, no reconciliation lag).
- **Objection (budget):** "No budget allocated this cycle." (David) — surfaced unprompted, a strong
  status-quo tell.
- **Risk:** the economic buyer is effectively a blocker; competitor remains the in-house build /
  analyst team, propped by the existing Clearwater spend.
- **Buying signal (champion-only):** Rebecca stayed engaged, pushed back on David in-room, and asked
  us to send a regulated-industry ROI case she could use.

## Commitments & next steps
- Cadence (alex-rep): follow up with a regulated-industry ROI case — forecast-miss and analyst-hours
  cost modelled in David's terms, against the ~$80k.
- Airwallex: David "will consider next cycle" — soft, undated, no owner-forcing follow-up booked.

## Coaching notes
- This was the call to win and we didn't. Rep talk ratio 0.58 with the economic buyer in the room is
  backwards — the buyer with the objection should be doing most of the talking. We talked past David's
  "analysts already do this" instead of into it: no reframe of the structural difference between
  software and an analyst team, and no attempt to dollarise the impact live while he was present. The
  next step ("consider next cycle") is a soft, undated non-commitment — a classic no-critical-event
  signal. Should have isolated David 1:1 before or right after this demo to reset the frame while it
  was still soft. This is the exact juncture the Employment Hero deal is now repeating.
