# Airwallex Discovery — 2026-01-14

**Attendees:** internal alex-rep | external Rebecca Hall (VP Sales)

## Summary
First discovery call, VP-Sales-level, brought in through Rebecca after she saw a Cadence forecast
teardown. Rebecca owns the revenue number at a regulated cross-border payments fintech but does not
own the forecasting tooling — that sits inside the CFO's finance org, which runs a ~6-person analyst
team building the forecast by hand in spreadsheets, with a Clearwater dashboard layer on top for exec
and board reporting. Real, specific pain came through at Rebecca's level; the open question by the end
was whether the economic buyer would ever feel it. He wasn't on this call, which in hindsight is the
first crack in the deal.

## SPICED captured this call
- **Situation:** Regulated cross-border payments fintech, multi-jurisdiction (APAC/EMEA). Forecasting
  is a finance-owned, in-house process: ~6 analysts, monthly bottoms-up spreadsheet forecast
  reconciled by hand against CRM data, Clearwater BI on top. Sales org ~45 reps. Rebecca owns the
  number, not the tool or the budget.
- **Pain:** "I feel the forecast pain, but here forecasting is seen as an analyst's job, not
  software." (Rebecca) She described quarterly forecasts landing ~15–20% off actuals, roll-ups eating
  three-plus days a month, and no clean rep-level pipeline hygiene view — she finds slippage after the
  quarter, not during it. "By the time the analysts reconcile it, the quarter's already gone."
  (Rebecca)
- **Impact (partial):** Framed but not yet dollarised — a blown quarter is a board-level surprise, and
  the manual roll-up burns analyst time. Rebecca believes it, but agreed the CFO would need it in his
  own terms.
- **Critical Event:** None surfaced. No board mandate, no deadline. "There's no gun to our head on
  this — it's a want, not a must, at least the way David will see it." (Rebecca)
- **Decision:** Rebecca is the champion; David Nguyen (CFO) is the economic buyer and, on her read,
  the skeptic. She flagged early that he views forecasting as something his analysts already deliver.

## Signals
- **Objection (early / cultural):** strong in-house-build bias — forecasting seen as an analyst
  deliverable, not a software category. Reinforced by the existing Clearwater dashboard investment.
- **Buying signal:** Rebecca actively championing; asked for an ROI framing she could take to David,
  and offered to test his appetite herself.
- **Risk flagged:** the economic buyer was not on the call and holds a status-quo frame; competitor is
  the in-house / analyst team incumbent.

## Commitments & next steps
- Cadence (alex-rep): build an ROI framing for a regulated environment — forecast-miss cost, analyst
  hours, board-surprise risk — sized against an ~$80k annual spend.
- Airwallex (Rebecca): test appetite with David (CFO) and try to get him onto the demo.

## Coaching notes
- Solid discovery — pain is real, specific, and quantifiable-in-principle at the champion's level, and
  the "analyst's job, not software" line names the whole deal thesis in one sentence. But two early
  risks were logged and not acted on hard enough: (1) the in-house-build bias is a category-level
  objection, not a feature gap, and (2) the economic buyer was absent and already skeptical. We should
  have pushed for direct EB access this call, or at minimum armed Rebecca with a dollar figure before
  David formed his view — not after. Probe the EB sooner; a champion who owns the number but not the
  budget can open the door but can't close it.
